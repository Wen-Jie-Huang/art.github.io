About

Between Science and Sensation

Wenjie Huang is a PhD student working in microalgae industrial biotechnology. His research traces how tiny photosynthetic organisms scale from laboratory bench to industrial reality — and along the way, he began noticing the aesthetic and philosophical vocabulary hidden in petri dishes, salt crystals, and algal blooms.

This site is a journal of those observations, moving between scientific rigor and artistic sensibility.

Research

Microalgae Industrial Biotechnology

From strain cultivation to process design, my work focuses on turning microscopic photosynthesis into viable industrial systems: cultivation optimization, downstream processing, and the environmental trade-offs of scaling up.

Life · Crystallization

A salt lake keeps time in crystals and eggs

A lake in Inner Mongolia turns its own evaporation into a calendar. As the brine retreats, salt grows around the tiny brine shrimp that have briefly turned the water red—then settles, holding their next generation dormant beneath a mineral crust.

Under the microscope, life and crystal share a frame. The work reads that frame as a quiet promise: dryness is temporary, return is certain.

Algae · Jelly

An unknown alga held in its own thickened garden

An alga was lifted out of an alkaline lake and put to live inside a jelly. It still breathes and photosynthesizes, but it can no longer drift. The thick medium becomes a frame, a garden, and a question at once.

The work keeps the alga nameless on purpose. We do not know everything that lives in our lakes, and the lakes do not owe us a list.

Dancing upon Algae

Spirulina choreographed by vibration

On an industrial pilot line, a vibro-sieve usually separates Spirulina from its brine. One afternoon, by changing the concentration and the density on the screen, the algae stopped separating and started to dance.

Each grain became a tiny dancer; the sieve became a stage. The work asks whether the difference between processing and performing is only a matter of speed, and attention.

Algae · Words

Microalgae writing on agar

On a petri dish, algae grow into characters, landscapes, and sentences. I do not design the strokes in advance—I set the concentration, the agar texture, the direction of one LED—and let the algae decide what to say.

The work is a collaboration between my protocol and their will to grow toward light. It keeps rewriting itself long after I have left the lab.
