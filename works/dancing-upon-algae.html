Dancing upon Algae

Spirulina choreographed by vibration

Year
2024
Medium
Photography, high-speed video, single-channel video loop
Dimensions
Variable; photographs 80 × 120 cm and 60 × 90 cm; video 2 min 30 sec loop, silent
Location
Spirulina harvest line, industrial pilot facility
Materials
Spirulina (Arthrospira platensis) culture, vibro-sieve separator, controlled culture density, ambient light
Edition
Photography: edition of 3 + 2 AP · Video: open edition

In industry, a vibro-sieve is a machine for separating solids from liquid. I kept it running every day for months. One afternoon, by adjusting the concentration of Spirulina and the density on the sieve, the algae stopped separating and started dancing. Each grain became a tiny dancer, and the sieve became the stage. This work asks whether the difference between 'processing' and 'performing' is only a matter of speed and attention.

Process · Technique

Filmed and photographed at the Spirulina pilot line under available light. The dance in each image and video is created live by varying feed concentration and sieve amplitude; nothing in the frame has been composited or repeated. Videos are single continuous takes, presented on loop.

Separation is the moment a thing is judged. In the factory, a vibro-sieve decides whether the Spirulina is dry enough, pure enough, ready for the next step. I stood beside it for months, watching green matter fall into bins, and thought of it as a verdict.

Then I changed the concentration. The sieve was still separating, but the algae on top were no longer merely passing through. They were hovering, sliding, meeting and parting in small green groups. The machine had not changed; the choreography had.

What the video keeps is not the function of the machine but the rhythm inside it. The algae do not know they are being harvested. They only know the vibration, the light, the pressure of other grains against them. From that limited knowledge, they make a dance.

I think of this as the opposite of documentation. Documentary photography usually steps back to show a process in its entirety. Here I stepped closer, until the process forgot it was being watched and became something else.
